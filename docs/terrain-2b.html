Phase 2B · implementation design

Seam-safe planetary terrain

A bounded, balanced planetary mesh that preserves coverage while its resolution changes. This page documents the runtime implementation, not the aspirational geology or atmosphere stack.

The stable recovery foundation is retained: reverse-Z depth, validated first-frame submission, visible GPU errors, and commit-isolated module/shader URLs. Phase 2B changes terrain topology and transitions. It does not implement physical atmosphere, surface physics or photoreal materials.

1. Contracts that may never be broken

Each cube face is the square [-1,1]². Selected leaves must partition that square without an ancestor and descendant being present together. The sum of leaf areas on each face is exactly four. Every edge neighbour differs by at most one subdivision level, including neighbours belonging to different cube faces. Tile-budget exhaustion may reduce detail; it must not remove coverage.

For every face: sum(tile.size²) = 4
For every touching edge: abs(levelA - levelB) ≤ 1
For every leaf: no selected ancestor exists
For every cut: leafCount ≤ logicalBudget
For every draw: instanceCount ≤ rendererCapacity

The browser currently uses a 1,536-leaf logical budget and a 4,096-instance render buffer. Logical and rendered tile counts differ while a transition is active. This is intentional, not a missing-tile symptom.

2. Cube topology and rotating frames

engine/planet/topology.js derives the 24 directed edge connections from the six cube bases. Connections record the neighbouring face, its touching edge and whether its varying coordinate is reversed. Integer tile addresses remain dyadic; a seam is not approximated using geographic distance or loose point matching.

FaceCube point at (u,v)
+X / 0(1,v,-u)
-X / 1(-1,v,u)
+Y / 2(u,1,-v)
-Y / 3(u,-1,v)
+Z / 4(u,v,1)
-Z / 5(-u,v,-1)

neighborAddress returns an equal-level neighbour cell. coveringLeaf searches that cell and its ancestors; neighborsOf descends only along the touching edge when it needs finer leaves. Cube corners therefore work without a separate corner exception. All three incident faces converge on the same corner direction.

The selector transforms the world-space camera into the rotating planet frame using the inverse rotation exactly once. The earlier extra negation is removed. Rotation-invariance tests compare equivalent camera poses under nonzero planetary rotations.

3. Atomic, budget-safe balancing

quadtree.js starts from all six roots. A max-heap prioritizes candidates by projected patch footprint with split/merge hysteresis. Before splitting a leaf, the selector recursively collects any coarser neighbours that must also split to preserve the 2:1 rule. That closure is an atomic transaction.

candidate + required neighbour splits → transaction
cost = 3 × numberOfSplitParents
if currentLeaves + cost ≤ budget:
    replace EVERY transaction parent by its four children
else:
    keep ALL transaction parents

A rejected transaction does not prevent another cheaper candidate from being examined. Coverage and balance are never traded away to spend the last available tile slots. A max-heap replaces the previous repeated whole-array maximum search. The final stitch mask belongs only to the finer side of a mixed-resolution edge.

The current priority is projected patch footprint, approximately radius × angular span × focal scale / distance. It is not yet a certified screen-space bound on terrain approximation error. Conservative geometric-error metadata, frustum/horizon culling and GPU selection remain future work. In particular, this release does not reinstate the earlier unsafe horizon rejection.

4. Geometric edge stitching, without skirts

patch-grid.js emits one 32×32-cell surface-only grid. The new indices have outward counter-clockwise winding on every face, so backface culling is enabled consistently. Vertical skirts and their duplicate border walls are gone.

On a fine edge next to a coarse tile, even fine-grid vertices coincide with the coarse grid. Odd boundary vertices are placed at the Cartesian midpoint of the displaced coarse endpoints. The midpoint is not normalized back onto the sphere: doing that would reopen a curvature gap, even if the terrain heights matched.

fineOddPosition = 0.5 × (coarsePositionA + coarsePositionB)
fineOddHeight   = 0.5 × (coarseHeightA + coarseHeightB)

The mask bits are north=1, east=2, south=4, west=8. Same-level edges receive no stitch. The same rule applies on internal face edges and cross-face seams. Fine collinear boundary vertices are retained; tiles remain independently instanced rather than welded into one mesh. Finite-precision raster agreement is validated separately from the mathematical continuity contract.

5. Coordinated full-position morphing

Independently morphing tile heights is insufficient: a coarse mesh has different Cartesian curvature and its boundary may itself be stitched. transitions.js retains the complete old and new balanced cuts and computes their common refinement. Every render patch identifies the old surface tile and new surface tile covering it.

old balanced cut ─┐ ├─ common-refinement render cut ─┬─ source surface new balanced cut ─┘ └─ target surface │ ONE global smoothstep alpha │ camera-relative position

For a vertex introduced by refinement, the source surface is reconstructed using barycentric interpolation of the source tile's actual triangle vertices. Those vertices already obey source-edge stitching. The target is reconstructed the same way. The renderer blends all three position components and the material height, not just radial displacement.

Transitions last 0.35 simulation seconds. Their endpoint cuts remain frozen during the blend. After completion the renderer compacts to the new stable cut, then considers camera changes that occurred during the transition. Normal selection is throttled to a 0.12-second interval outside transitions. This bounds update work but can briefly delay new refinement after a fast manoeuvre; it never creates a missing branch.

For two complete cuts on the same six trees, the common refinement has at most oldCount + newCount − 6 leaves. Reserving two logical budgets therefore leaves sufficient draw capacity. There is also an explicit capacity guard: an unaffordable transition keeps the previous complete cut instead of silently truncating uploaded instances.

6. WebGPU data and shader evaluation

Vertex locationData
0Shared grid vertex: u, v, reserved, reserved
1Render tile: face, u0, v0, size
2Render metadata: level, x, y, stitchMask
3Source tile: u0, v0, size, stitchMask
4Target tile: u0, v0, size, stitchMask

The render record is 16 floats / 64 bytes. The logical-cut serializer remains an 8-float inspection format and must not be confused with the renderer ABI. uploadInstances validates the requested stride, finiteness and capacity and uploads the exact TypedArray view without the optional size argument.

The geometry shader constructs a bounded linear sampling stencil before evaluating procedural terrain. The upper bound is 24 samples: two union-edge endpoints, two morph endpoints, three triangle vertices and two source-edge endpoints. Most unchanged interior vertices need just one sample. Terrain evaluation has one dynamic call site, avoiding multiplication of the expensive fractal-noise program through nested helper inlining. Weighted positions are accumulated in camera-relative space.

Production material brightness no longer depends on LOD. The optional tile-grid overlay is a debugging view, not part of normal shading. Radial lighting normals and the atmospheric rim approximation remain unchanged in scope.

7. Tests and release gate

npm run check executes syntax checks and both engine and terrain behavioural tests. Topology tests cover all directed edges and reversed mappings through level 12, outward triangle winding, complete face areas, absence of ancestor overlap, tiny/saturated budgets, 2:1 balance, rotating-frame invariance, morph endpoint identity and cross-face chord continuity through intermediate alpha values.

The CPU geometric oracle uses independent double-precision interpolation and a non-flat analytic height field. Its tight continuity tolerances are not a claim of nanometre GPU accuracy. The actual WGSL path is exercised separately in Chromium/WebGPU.

browser-smoke.mjs checks production shading, initial startup, hard reload, resize, close orbit, reset, bad shaders and unsupported WebGPU. terrain-browser.mjs uses the unchanged production vertex shader with a flat fragment coverage color to distinguish true gaps from dark oceans or the night side. It samples intermediate morph states, both sides of all 12 cube edges, all eight corners, descent/ascent and budgets down to the six roots. Nadir views must exceed 99.9% viewport coverage; full missing tiles fail the test.

Both browser suites run before Pages publication. Screenshots, validation errors, build identity and coverage measurements are stored in GitHub Actions artifacts. Software rendering validates correctness, not a hardware frame-rate target. A passed test matrix does not certify every browser, GPU or camera pose.

8. Explicit boundaries and next work

This is the seam-safe terrain milestone, not a finished planetary renderer. It has no asynchronous terrain residency cache, no physical sky-scattering lookup tables, no local collision bubble and no terrain-clearance landing logic. The maximum LOD remains 12. High-precision tile-local vertex origins, terrain-derived normals, richer geological materials, certified geometric-error bounds and ground-level detail remain separate work.

The demo's Orbit, 333 km, Face seam, Cube corner and 10 km controls are inspection presets. R resets orbit; L toggles the tile grid. For reproducible developer tests, ?diagnostics&lodBudget=96 exposes a limited pose/budget/morph inspection API. Default users do not need it.
