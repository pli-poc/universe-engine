Surface realism

Phase 2C improves the precision and material response of the existing seamless planetary mesh without introducing atmosphere, ocean simulation or landing physics.

Tile-local camera anchors

Every rendered quadtree patch carries a camera-relative anchor computed on the CPU from Float64 planet/camera state. WGSL reconstructs vertices relative to that anchor. The current shader still forms planet-sized f32 positions before subtracting the anchor, so this is not yet a fully cancellation-free local evaluation path.

Float64 camera ↔ planet ↓ Float64 tile surface anchor ↓ cast once camera-relative f32 anchor + WGSL delta ↓ clip-space projection

No centimetre precision is claimed: tile size, shader arithmetic, physics and collision have their own error budgets. The noise fix below leaves the tested vertex, stitching and morphing paths unchanged.

Normals and procedural detail

The fragment shader derives the geometric normal from derivatives of the final stitched/morphed camera-relative position. The original unfiltered screen-space noise perturbation was too strong and produced speckles. Detail now uses the analytic gradient of quintic lattice noise, projected onto the geometric tangent plane. The sum is bounded to a dimensionless slope of 0.18 (about ten degrees), and basin placeholders receive a smaller response. It changes light response rather than generating extra triangles.

Pixel-footprint filtering

The renderer estimates metres per pixel from derivatives of the surface direction. Four procedural layers use lattice-cell widths of 2048, 256, 32 and 4 metres, with slope amplitudes 0.08, 0.06, 0.035 and 0.02. A layer fades smoothly from full weight at 0.125 cells per pixel to zero at 0.5. Thus orbit views retain large-scale form and material colour without unresolved normal noise; lower flight reveals only the detail that the pixels can resolve.

This is a conservative procedural level-of-detail filter, not exact signal reconstruction. No full-screen blur is applied. Terrain shape, geometry LOD, stitching, morphing and the existing material palette are unchanged. Full temporal reconstruction and variance-aware specular antialiasing remain future work.

Body-fixed coordinates

Material fields and normal-detail gradients are sampled in the planet's rotating body frame, not camera or inertial coordinates. Lighting normals are transformed back into the lighting frame. Moving the camera changes the sampling footprint, not the identity of the material.

Procedural material model

Basin, soil, rock and snow weights are derived from elevation, slope, latitude and deterministic procedural fields. Those weights blend base colour and roughness. The surface is currently treated as a dielectric; metallic materials are intentionally excluded from natural terrain.

PBR lighting

The direct-light model uses a GGX microfacet distribution, Smith visibility and Schlick Fresnel with a dielectric F0. Diffuse energy is reduced by the Fresnel term. A small ambient term and the existing temporary atmospheric rim remain until Phase 3 replaces the rim with physical scattering.

HDR and display transform

Lighting is evaluated in linear floating point inside the fragment shader. Exposure is applied before an ACES-style fitted tone map and an explicit linear-to-sRGB conversion. The current pass outputs directly to the canvas: it does not yet store an intermediate HDR render target for atmospheric composition.

Debug views

The demo exposes Beauty, Normals, Roughness, Materials and Height modes. These are runtime shader views, not separate mockups. With ?diagnostics, window.__astravaDebug.setDetailStrength(0) disables detail for an A/B comparison and setDetailStrength(1) restores it. The hook validates the range [0,1].

Low-altitude safety

Free flight is clamped outside planet radius + configured maximum terrain height + 500 m. The “1 km clearance” preset sits one kilometre above the configured maximum terrain envelope, not one kilometre above the actual ground at that location. This is deliberately conservative and is not terrain collision or landing physics.

Not included

  • Physical atmospheric scattering and aerial perspective.
  • A separate ocean surface, Fresnel water or waves.
  • Generated material/normal tile residency and caching.
  • Tectonic, hydraulic or erosion simulation.
  • Collision, landing gear, walking or centimetre-scale physics.

Shading-quality checks

The quick/full browser smoke profiles now compare rendered images from an identical frozen pose with detail enabled and disabled. At orbit, differences must be below 0.5 mean and 4 maximum 8-bit channel levels. At 13 km reference-radius altitude, the normal-debug pass must retain some resolved detail while staying below 12.5 degrees of perturbation, allowing for image quantisation.

The full profile also recompiles the original noisy detail function as a negative control and verifies that it exceeds the orbit error limit. This prevents a test that merely accepts any colourful planet. Images and surface-quality.json are saved with the existing WebGPU smoke evidence. Geometry coverage thresholds are not relaxed by this fix; the public delivery profile remains small.

Sources: WGSL derivative built-ins and Mikkelsen, Surface Gradient-Based Bump Mapping Framework. The filter thresholds and slope budget are Astrava engineering choices.

Validation

The existing Phase 2B topology/morph suite still gates publication. Production WebGPU smoke and delivery verification remain mandatory for renderer changes. Shading metrics, invalid-normal rejection and the shader filtering contract also have fast unit tests.
