Phase 2C · baseline implemented

Surface realism

Phase 2C improves the precision and material response of the existing seamless planetary mesh without introducing atmosphere, ocean simulation or landing physics.

Tile-local camera anchors

Every rendered quadtree patch carries a camera-relative anchor computed on the CPU from Float64 planet/camera state. WGSL reconstructs vertices as a small local delta from the patch anchor. This avoids repeatedly subtracting two planet-radius-sized f32 values for nearby surface vertices.

Float64 camera ↔ planet ↓ Float64 tile surface anchor ↓ cast once camera-relative f32 anchor + small local WGSL delta ↓ clip-space projection

This materially improves low-altitude precision, but it is not a claim of centimetre precision: tile size, shader arithmetic, physics and collision have their own error budgets.

Normals and procedural detail

The fragment shader derives the geometric normal from derivatives of the final stitched/morphed camera-relative position. A small procedural field perturbs that response at a finer frequency. It changes light response rather than generating extra triangles.

Procedural material model

Basin, soil, rock and snow weights are derived from elevation, slope, latitude and deterministic procedural fields. Those weights blend base colour and roughness. The surface is currently treated as a dielectric; metallic materials are intentionally excluded from natural terrain.

PBR lighting

The direct-light model uses a GGX microfacet distribution, Smith visibility and Schlick Fresnel with a dielectric F0. Diffuse energy is reduced by the Fresnel term. A small ambient term and the existing temporary atmospheric rim remain until Phase 3 replaces the rim with physical scattering.

HDR and display transform

Lighting is accumulated in linear HDR. Exposure is applied before an ACES-style fitted tone map and an explicit linear-to-sRGB conversion. This establishes a consistent imaging path for the later physical sun/atmosphere work.

Debug views

The demo exposes Beauty, Normals, Roughness, Materials and Height modes. They are runtime shader views, not separate mockups, and are intended to make material and seam problems diagnosable before adding another visual subsystem.

Low-altitude safety

Free flight is clamped outside planet radius + configured maximum terrain height + 500 m. The “1 km clearance” preset sits one kilometre above the configured maximum terrain envelope. This is deliberately conservative and is not terrain collision or landing physics.

Not included

  • Physical atmospheric scattering and aerial perspective.
  • A separate ocean surface, Fresnel water or waves.
  • Generated material/normal tile residency and caching.
  • Tectonic, hydraulic or erosion simulation.
  • Collision, landing gear, walking or centimetre-scale physics.

Validation

The existing Phase 2B topology/morph suite still gates publication because the vertex ABI changed. Phase 2C adds contract tests for anchor precision, the expanded instance ABI and required PBR/HDR shader stages. Production WebGPU smoke and delivery verification remain mandatory for renderer changes.
